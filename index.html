What to send from your flight computer

      
Send one reading per line, 5 to 20 times a second, ending in a newline. Either plain CSV in this order:

      t_ms,lat,lon,alt_m,speed_mps,ax_g,ay_g,az_g,batt_v,sats

      or JSON lines such as {"t_ms":1200,"lat":40.1,"lon":-100.2,"alt":152.3,"v":61.2,"batt":3.9,"sats":9}.
      You can also send a header line first with your own column names (lat, lon, alt, speed, batt and so on) and any fields you leave out are simply skipped.
      USB works in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Bluetooth works with ESP32 or HM-10 style modules. For a radio link, plug the receiver of your LoRa or telemetry
      radio into the computer by USB and connect to that. Open the page from your GitHub Pages address, not an embedded preview, or the browser may block USB, Bluetooth and the camera.

About AeroForge

    
An educational rocket-design platform: design a rocket, see the engineering consequences
      immediately, and learn the reasoning behind them.

    

      
You design it
AeroForge never silently changes your rocket. It analyses what
        you built, flags what might not work, and explains why — the decision stays yours.

      
Explain, don't just fail
Every warning says what the problem is, why it matters
        physically, and what kind of change would address it.

      
Honest numbers
Calculations use real engineering relationships, simplified where
        that keeps things understandable. Every simplification is written down.

      
Yours, locally
No account, no sign-up. Projects and progress are stored in your
        own browser.

    

    

      
What the simulator does and doesn't model

      
Included: shaped thrust curve scaled to the motor's rated impulse, drag with an
      altitude-dependent air density, mass loss as propellant burns, and parachute descent. Not included: wind
      during ascent, angle of attack, transonic drag rise, fin flutter, and the exact measured thrust trace of
      your specific motor. Stability uses a simplified nose-and-fins method, not full Barrowman.
      Treat results as estimates for comparing designs, not flight predictions.

How the numbers are estimated

    

      
Important: these are estimates from simplified formulas. They have not been validated against real flights.

      
Flight: every 10 ms the simulator adds up thrust, weight and drag, divides by the current mass to get acceleration, and steps speed and height forward.

      
Drag: ½ × air density × speed² × drag coefficient × frontal area, with density falling off with altitude.

      
Stability: the distance between the centre of gravity and a simplified centre of pressure, expressed in body diameters.

      
Flight computer: battery life is capacity divided by total current draw, and the pyro pulse sags the voltage by current times the battery’s internal resistance.

    

    
Safety first

    

      
AeroForge is a design and learning tool. It does not replace a launch safety code. Use only commercially made motors, never build your own propellant,
      fly in an open area, and follow your local rocketry club’s rules and your country’s laws. Simulated results are estimates, not guarantees.

    

    
Questions

    

      
Can the assistant really do anything?
It can do anything the app supports: change any design setting, take the rocket apart, zoom into parts, rotate the view, run the simulation, save, and switch pages. It also answers rocketry questions. Anything outside the app it will tell you it cannot do.

      
Is my data uploaded anywhere?
Projects and progress stay in your own browser. Only the text you type to the assistant is sent for it to answer.

      
How good are the numbers?
They are rough estimates from a simplified model. They have not been checked against real flight data, so they are not measurements and should not be used to decide whether a launch is safe. The model leaves out wind during ascent, angle of attack, transonic drag, fin flutter and more. Use it to compare designs and to learn.

      
Why is my rocket flagged as unstable?
The centre of pressure is too close to, or ahead of, the centre of gravity. Add fin area, sweep the fins back or move weight forward, then check the margin again.

      
Why does AeroForge need a barometer to deploy a parachute?
This is specific to how AeroForge models the flight computer, not a rule for every rocket. In this app, deployment is triggered when the barometer detects apogee, so the simulator and the generated code will not deploy without one. Real rockets can use other methods, such as accelerometer-based detection, a timer, or the motor's own ejection charge, and each has its own trade-offs.
